Your first month

What happens after your parents leave?

A first month is mostly ordinary moments adding up until the place starts to feel like yours.
Move-in weekend

Land. Unpack. Meet people.

Find your room, learn the building, meet your floormates and RA, figure out where to eat, and take the first walk around campus before you need to know where anything is.

✓ Room set up enough to sleep
✓ Dining hall located
✓ First familiar faces acquired
Week 1

Classes start. So does the city.

Walk your class routes, try the library, make the first grocery run, get downtown, and discover that Halifax is compact enough to start learning quickly.

✓ Campus route feels normal
✓ Student card/U-Pass in use
✓ One favourite coffee spot found
Week 2

Your routine starts forming.

You know who is usually in the lounge, which dining hall meal you actually like, where you study best, and which clubs, teams or societies might be worth joining.

✓ First club or activity tried
✓ Laundry successfully survived
✓ Study rhythm emerging
Weeks 3–4

It begins to feel like home.

The city stops being a map. Residence stops being “the building.” You have people to text, places you recognize and a sense of how your life here might work.

✓ Weekend plans exist
✓ Someone knows your coffee order
✓ Calling home becomes optional, not urgent
Residence life

The practical stuff, without the mystery.

🍽 Food

Traditional residences require meal plans, which means food is part of the routine from day one rather than another logistical problem to solve.

🧺 Laundry & basics

Laundry, study rooms, shared spaces and front-desk support become part of the weekly rhythm. The page should show these plainly, because somebody eventually has to wash socks.

🤝 People nearby

Residence creates repeated contact with other first-year students. That matters more than another university slogan about belonging.

📚 Close to class

Howe, Shirreff, Risley and LeMarchant Place sit on Studley Campus; Gerard is downtown by Sexton and within walking distance of Studley.

🛟 Support

Residence staff, front desks and student supports give first-year students places to ask questions before small problems become dramatic family group-chat events.

🏙 Halifax outside the door

Campus is embedded in the city. Residence is a base for learning Halifax, not a bubble you need a car to escape.

Arriving from away

One trip. One room. A clear landing path.

For students coming from Ontario, Alberta, British Columbia or farther away, residence removes one of the biggest unknowns in moving to Halifax: where you are going to live when you arrive.
