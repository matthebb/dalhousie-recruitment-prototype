Here when you need us

Moving away should feel exciting. Not unsupported.

University brings new freedom, new people and new challenges. Most students will need help with something at some point. Dal has people, services and coordinated support to help you stay healthy, find your footing, solve problems and keep moving forward.

One university. Many kinds of support.

You do not need to know which office to call before something happens.

Academic, health, wellbeing, safety, accessibility and belonging supports exist across Dal. When a situation crosses several of those lines, Dal's Student Support Team can help coordinate a care-first response and connect students to the right people.

The support network

What “Dal's got you” actually means

Not one giant office. A connected set of people and services that become useful at different moments in university life.

🩺

Physical health

On-campus access to nurses, physicians and health services, with student health-plan support.

🧠

Mental health

Counselling, same-day options, peer support, psychiatry referrals and 24/7 external supports.

📚

Academic guidance

Faculty advising, Student Success Centres, tutoring and coaching when the academic path feels unclear.

♿

Accessibility

Advisors help identify accommodations, reduce learning barriers and connect students with appropriate resources.

🛡️

Safety

Campus security, emergency support, sexualized-violence resources, ombudsperson services and coordinated safety response.

🤝

Belonging

Community-specific advising and programming, student societies, peer networks, residence communities and spaces to find your people.

When support matters

Usually, it is not one dramatic moment.

Confidence comes from knowing ordinary problems have somewhere to go before they become bigger ones.

“I'm falling behind.”Start with advising, coaching, tutoring or accessibility support.
“I'm not doing great.”Health and counselling services can help you figure out what kind of support makes sense.
“I haven't found my people.”Residence, societies, identity-based supports, peer programs and campus involvement create multiple entry points.
“This is getting complicated.”The Student Support Team can help coordinate when wellbeing, safety, belonging and academics overlap.
For students

Independence does not mean doing everything alone.

One of the things university teaches is when to solve a problem yourself, when to ask for help, and how to use the people and systems around you well.

See your first month at Dal
For families

There is a support system on the other end of the move.

Families do not need a promise that nothing difficult will happen. They need confidence that students will have access to health care, mental-health support, advising, safety resources and people whose job is to notice when more coordinated support is needed.

Student privacy and autonomy still matter. Support at university is designed to help students build independence while ensuring help is available when it is needed.
A softer landing

Come for the opportunity. Know there are people here when life gets messy.

For students moving across the province, across Canada or across the world, confidence is not only about the program or the city. It is also knowing there is a place to live, a path into community and a support system behind you.
